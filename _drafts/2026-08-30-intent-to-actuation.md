### The Side-Effect Fallacy: Actuation is the Entire Point

> The software industry has a bizarre, solipsistic habit of treating real-world interaction as an awkward footnote called a "side effect."

Developers retreat into pure in-memory loops, congratulating themselves on stateless beauty, as if touching the physical world were an act of architectural vulgarity.

Let’s stop dicking around: **if there is no side effect, there is no point to the software.**

You didn't boot the computer to keep the CPU warm; you booted it to change the state of reality. The side effect is not a byproduct—it is the **actuation**.

* **The Solipsistic Trap:** Building massive, abstract castles in the sky that compute endlessly but produce fragile, un-trackable real-world outcomes.
* **The Bounded Actuation Cycle:**
1. **Intent:** Captured as a bounded, deterministic brief.
2. **Execution:** The compiler runs, the tests hold, the renderer evaluates.
3. **Actuation:** The artifact is committed to disk—briefs archived, ADRs recorded, markdown rendered to the matrix, knowledge transferred.
