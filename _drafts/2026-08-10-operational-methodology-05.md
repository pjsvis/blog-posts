In NASA culture, the phrase **"all going well"** carries a deep, highly nuanced, and historied operational meaning that aligns directly with your second interpretation—and takes it a step further.

To the outside world, "all going well" sounds like a passive, optimistic wish for smooth sailing. In aerospace engineering and flight operations, however, **there is no such thing as passive luck.**

### 1. The NASA Reality: The Nominal vs. The Off-Nominal

In NASA flight dynamics and mission control jargon, "all going well" translates to: **"The system is operating within nominal parameters, and all observed off-nominal trends remain within our margin of control."**

Your two-point breakdown captures the core mechanics:

1. **"Nothing bad happens"** $\rightarrow$ *Nominal Execution:* The physics of the trajectory, spacecraft telemetry, and environmental variables are tracking within predicted statistical bounds.
2. **"Nothing happens that we can't handle"** $\rightarrow$ *Contained Anomaly:* Minor anomalies or unexpected sensor readings occur, but they fall squarely inside pre-tested contingency procedures (Flight Rules) or active error margins.

### 2. The Nuanced Historical Philosophy: "High-Reliability" Culture

Historically, NASA learned through bitter tragedy (Apollo 1, Challenger, Columbia) that assuming "everything is fine" just because nothing has blown up yet is the most dangerous trap in engineering. They call this **Normalization of Deviance**—accepting small anomalies as normal simply because they haven't caused a failure *yet*.

Because of this, when NASA flight directors (from Gene Kranz in the Apollo era to modern Mars rover operations) use or imply "all going well," it is grounded in three historical operational principles:

* **Informed Confidence, Not Hope:** "All going well" never means "we hope nothing goes wrong." It means **"we have tested the boundaries, we are actively measuring the state of the system, and current telemetry confirms our assumptions."**
* **The Presence of Margins:** A mission is only "going well" if there is sufficient fuel, thermal overhead, communication bandwidth, and time remaining to execute a abort or contingency maneuver if needed. If margins evaporate, the mission is no longer "going well," even if the spacecraft is still technically flying.
* **Bounded Uncertainty:** Spaceflight is inherently non-deterministic and hazardous. NASA engineers know they cannot eliminate risk; they can only **bound uncertainty**. "All going well" means the system's current entropy is contained within known, actionable bounds.
