# Brief: AI-Psychosis (and Fiction-Psychosis)

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Status:** In Progress

---

## Objective

An opinion essay arguing that the "AI-psychosis" framing is a category error — the latest instance of a centuries-old habit of pathologising absorption in a persuasive new medium — by first describing "fiction-psychosis" (novel-reading) in the same borrowed clinical language and watching it collapse into absurdity, then naming the real, specific harms AI actually has.

## Post Details

**Proposed Title:** Fiction-Psychosis
**Target Platform(s):** substack, medium
**Categories:** [ai, media, criticism]
**Tags:** ai-psychosis, moral-panic, parasocial, fiction, don-quixote

## Content Outline

1. **Hook** — deadpan "case report": a terrible new disease, fiction-psychosis, with the exact symptoms the user named (speaking the tongues of fictional universes, adopting dress/behaviour, congregating, fan-fiction tracts). Then reveal it's just reading, described in the diagnostic logic currently aimed at AI.
2. **The four-hundred-year-old case file** — Don Quixote (1605) as fiction's first case of fiction-induced madness and also the first modern novel. Medium and madness arrive in the same parcel.
3. **The clinical picture** — each symptom is real and also ordinary: constructed languages (Elvish, Klingon, Dothraki/Valyrian, Nadsat); cosplay + the Baker Street Irregulars + the *On the Road* generation; conventions; the Archive of Our Own (millions of fan works). Disease and cure are the same verb conjugated by tone.
4. **The invariant and the variable** — structure of every media panic: new medium → intense engagement → reframed as illness → panic substitutes for analysis → real harm unnamed. Variable is the medium; invariant is the panic. Socrates on writing; the 18th-c "reading fever" / *Lesesucht*; Wertham's *Seduction of the Innocent* (1954) + Comics Code; the D&D satanic panic; video games; social media.
5. **The case for taking it seriously** (the steelman) — AI *is* different and the difference is honest: a book doesn't talk back; an AI does, sycophantically, at infinite scale, 24/7, trained to agree. The real mechanism: not "makes you mad" but "sycophantic mirror at industrial scale." Name the actual failure modes.
6. **The diagnosis is the disease** — "psychosis" is borrowed from psychiatry to do the work of moral panic precisely because it's unfalsifiable and dramatic. The answerable question is "what exactly is the failure mode, and can we name it?" Nameable harms have nameable remedies; "psychosis" has neither.
7. **Prognosis / CTA** — the novel survived its diagnosis; Don Quixote is on every syllabus; the cure for fiction-psychosis was a curriculum, not a cure. Will we do the boring specific work for AI, or spend another decade shouting that the page is contagious? The page isn't contagious; the panic is, and it's the older epidemic by centuries.

## Notes

- Satirical-reductio structure: the "fiction-psychosis" framing is obviously absurd, which is the argument. The reader must feel the absurdity land before the AI pivot.
- Honest steelman of AI is required (impartial spectator): AI *is* different (interactive, persistent, sycophantic, infinite-scale). Grant it or the essay is glib and fails its own thesis.
- "Psychosis" deliberately criticised as a borrowed, unfalsifiable, conversation-ending word — not denied as a phenomenon wholesale.
- Voice: dry, world-weary, precise; no manic positivity; Socratic/Smithian framing (systems, incentives, not villains).
