## 5. Mathematical Formulation of the Fascial Metric Tensor

To formalize the decentralization of the coordinate transformation engine from the cerebellum to the mechanical substrate, we map the structural state of the body using the classical tensor notation of Pellionisz and Llinás, modified for a continuous, material tensegrity network.

### 5.1 The Coordinate Space ($\mathbf{\Omega}$)

### 5.2 The Covariant Sensory Tensor ($A_i$)

The covariant components represent the internal measurement of structural deformation (strain, shear, and localized pressure) acting along the oblique fascial axes.

$$A_i = \mathbf{J} \cdot \mathbf{e}_i$$

Where $\mathbf{J}$ is the global mechanical load vector acting on the tissue segment, and the covariance signifies that the measurement is dependent on the local geometry of the fascial base vectors. In practice, this represents the physical deformation of the interstitial Ruffini and mechanoreceptor membranes embedded within those specific fiber lines.

### 5.3 The Contravariant Tension Tensor ($A^i$)

The contravariant components represent the real-time kinetic tension (pre-stress) allocated along the structural lines of force to maintain structural integrity or protective armoring:

$$\mathbf{T} = A^i \mathbf{e}_i$$

Where $\mathbf{T}$ is the total tension vector expressed by the muscle-fascia unit. These components dictate the actual physical pull exerted across the joints and segments.

### 5.4 The Fascial Metric Tensor ($g_{ij}$)

In classical Tensor Network Theory, the metric tensor is a neural calculation executed by the cerebellum. In this extension, the metric tensor **is the physical state of the material substrate**.

The fascial metric tensor ($g_{ij}$) characterizes the local geometric and material properties of the connective tissue matrix—specifically its thickness, collagen density, and the thixotropic viscosity ($\eta$) of the hyaluronan fluid layer:

$$g_{ij} = \mathbf{e}_i \cdot \mathbf{e}_j \cdot f(\eta)$$

$$A^i = g^{ij} A_j$$
