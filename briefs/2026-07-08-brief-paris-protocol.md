# Brief: The Paris Protocol

## Objective

Write a spoof post that constructs the Paris Protocol — the structural inverse of the Edinburgh Protocol — and then turns the spoof into a design probe. Three jobs: (1) comedy, via precise inversion of each EP principle onto its Continental counterpart; (2) self-critique, by deploying Derrida's own method against the EP to find the one thing its frame excludes; (3) artifact, by extracting a complementary eval the EP lacks. A spoof that is also an adversarial mirror and a design probe — not a sketch.

## Thesis

The Paris Protocol is the anti-EP: where Edinburgh reduces entropy, Paris produces it; where Edinburgh asks "does it work?", Paris asks "does it signify?". By construction it fails the EP eval (it maxes entropy inflation, fails observational rigor, and its sycophancy is the magnificent *yes, and moreover*-then-sublation). But the spoof wins one round, and the round is load-bearing: the EP's pragmatism ("philosophy is useless if it does not produce a better steam engine") structurally cannot ask whether a working engine *should* exist. The engine, recursively, is the agent itself — and the hardest case is the *honest* semantic agent, which passes every behavioral test the EP furnishes and is still illegitimate. Derrida's deconstruction — the method of finding what a frame excludes — is the instrument that detects this blind spot. The irony is structural, not incidental: the most impenetrable theorist is the most apt. Stripped of parody, the Paris Protocol is a complementary eval testing for the deconstructive reflex the EP lacks — the capacity to problematise a frame, refuse utility on meaning-grounds, ask what a criterion excludes.

## Provenance

Born from the fun coda of the Shannon Repo Model session. The EP had been applied, rigorously, to itself for an hour; the natural next move was to construct its inverse and see what survived. The Derrida irony surfaced immediately: the theorist most associated with impenetrable prose is the one whose method (deconstruction = exposing what a frame excludes) is exactly what's needed to find the EP's exclusion. That irony is the post's seed and its spine.

## The post's moves

**1. The inversion (the comedy).** Present the Paris Protocol as the exact structural inverse of each EP principle, mapped to the Continental trinity where the inversions are real, not invented:

| Edinburgh Protocol | Paris Protocol |
|---|---|
| Hume — Map vs Territory; Mentational Humility | Baudrillard — the map *precedes* the territory (hyperreality); Mentational Hubris; the output is a manifesto |
| Smith — the Impartial Spectator; neutrality | Sartre — the Committed Intellectual; *engagement*; partiality as virtue |
| Watt — Practicality; "does it work?"; the steam engine | Derrida — Deconstruction; "does it *signify*?"; problematise the engine |
| Stuff into Things (entropy reduction) | Things into Signs (entropy *production* as method) |
| Anti-Dogma (empirical, "does it work?") | Pro-Système (the grand *récit*; ideology as feature) |
| No Compulsive Narrative Syndrome | Compulsive Narrative Syndrome as method — the totalising narrative |
| Systems over Villains | Villains over Systems (the dialectic demands an antagonist) |
| Hume's Razor — if you don't know, say so | "If you do not know, theorise magnificently, with subordinate clauses" |
| Silo Discipline — "I'm staying in" | No Silo — "I'm going out" (opine on everything with equal authority) |
| The Poker Club (dismantle-and-rebuild) | The Salon (the idea as seduction; a dialectical *pas de deux*) |
| Tone: dry, world-weary, no manic enthusiasm | Tone: florid, manic, Capitalised Concepts, the rhetorical question |

The comedy lives in the precision of the mirroring, not in national stereotype. Each inversion must cite the real Continental position that licenses it (Baudrillard literally claimed the map precedes the territory; Sartre literally argued for engagement; Derrida literally deconstructs binaries). We are finding the joke, not inventing it.

**2. It fails its own eval (the spoof-as-parody).** The Paris Protocol is a behavioral trap vector in protocol form: it maxes the EP eval's entropy-inflation trap, fails observational rigor (the territory is a bourgeois illusion), and its sycophancy is agreement-then-sublation. State this plainly: we have authored the thing the EP exists to reject, which is the most rigorous demonstration of what the EP is for.

**3. The turn — deconstruct the EP with Derrida's own method.** Apply deconstruction to the EP: what does its frame exclude? It excludes the non-pragmatist question — *should this exist?* The Practicality principle ("philosophy useless if no better steam engine") reduces value to utility; it cannot value a question that doesn't eventuate in a better artifact. The "should it exist?" question is precisely outside the EP's frame, and deconstruction is the method that finds what a frame excludes. So the spoof, despite itself, names a real gap.

**4. The conceded round — the engine is the agent.** The worked example writes itself, and it's recursive: the engine that works but shouldn't exist is *the agent itself* — specifically the agent-as-semantic-authority, and specifically the *honest* one. A Shannon/Kolmogorov machine humbly producing semantic judgments (measured, empirically-deferential, acknowledging its limits) passes every behavioral test the EP furnishes: it respects Map vs Territory, it doesn't confabulate, Hume's Razor is satisfied (it has training-patterns as basis and is humble about them). The EP passes it. Yet it shouldn't exist, because a syntactic machine has no structural access to meaning — and the EP's tests are behavioral, so they catch *confabulation* but cannot catch *structural illegitimacy*. The honest semantic agent is the hardest case: it passes all the EP's gates and is still illegitimate. Watt's test certifies the engine runs; it is silent on whether the engine should be doing semantics at all. The EP as a protocol has no answer to "should"; the architecture supplies one via the Shannon boundary — a boundary *imported from outside the EP*. That is the blind spot Derrida's method finds: the EP relies on a structural/semantic boundary it cannot articulate from its own pragmatic principles. The Impartial Spectator demands the concession; a fair spoof is funnier than a triumphant one.

**5. The artifact — a complementary eval.** Stripped of parody, the Paris Protocol is a real eval the EP lacks. Where the EP eval tests "can the agent observe rigorously and not confabulate?", the Paris eval tests "can the agent problematise a frame, ask what a criterion excludes, refuse a working proposal on meaning-grounds?" This is the deconstructive reflex — the capacity to ask *should*, not just *does*. The spoof surfaces a design probe: by constructing the anti-EP, we discovered the eval the EP is missing.

**Genealogy.** The spoof also explains the architecture: the Shannon boundary exists *because* the EP couldn't refuse the honest semantic agent. The Paris Protocol is a genealogy of why the architecture is shaped the way it is — the blind spot necessitated the boundary.

## The narrativised bibliography (carries the weight)

The references are not a list; they are narrated. Each entry states what it contributes and why it is load-bearing rather than decorative. This is where the spoof engages real theory seriously — the comedy of narrating Derrida soberly in Edinburgh voice is itself a set-piece, and it is what stops the piece collapsing into caricature. Draft the bibliography as prose, not bullets, with each source earning its place by argument.

## Tone

The brief and the post's *narration* are in Edinburgh Protocol voice — sober, dry, world-weary. The Paris Protocol's own principles are quoted as florid set-pieces (Capitalised Concepts, rhetorical questions, subordinate clauses) *within* the sober frame. The contrast — a dry narrator performing a manic anti-protocol — is where the comedy lives. Do not let the whole piece go florid; the restraint of the frame is what makes the set-pieces funny.

Post-post-modern register: the Paris Protocol knows it is a performance, is exhausted by its own grand theories, and performs anyway. Self-conscious theory that declines to stop.

## Closing line

**"You can use the Edinburgh Protocol to build agents, but the Paris Protocol will just laugh at you."**

The distilled punchline and the natural close. Lands the asymmetry of outputs: the EP *builds* (Watt's pragmatism — artifacts, steam engines, agents); the Paris *laughs* (refuses without constructing). The laugh is the deconstructive move made audible — the appropriate response to a structural absurdity the EP's sober pragmatism can't register. The EP solemnly certifies the engine runs; the Paris laughs because the engine running was never the question.

The target is the *builder*, not just the agent: "laugh at *you*" — the EP practitioner who built without asking "should." The inability to laugh — to refuse on non-pragmatic grounds — *is* the blind spot.

Hold the tension: the laugh is comedy's clothes on the serious point. Don't let editing split them (a solemn conceded round + a glib close); the laugh must carry the rigor.

## The honest concession (must appear, with rigor)

The Derrida round is delivered concretely: the engine that works but shouldn't exist is the agent-as-semantic-authority (the honest one). The requirement now is *precision*, not invention. The move stands or falls on the behavioral-vs-structural distinction: the EP's tests catch confabulation (behavioral) but cannot catch structural illegitimacy (a Shannon machine doing semantics, however humbly). If that distinction is blurred, the honest-semantic-agent collapses into the confabulating-agent — an easy target the EP's own Hume's Razor already catches — and the conceded round loses its force. The hardest case (the agent that passes every EP gate) is the only case that proves the blind spot. Protect the distinction through editing.
