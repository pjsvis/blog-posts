Your hypothesis hits on a well-documented sociolinguistic and historical phenomenon: Russian public discourse operates under intense linguistic hedging, circumlocution, and Aesopian language.

In the street interviews conducted by 1420 ([Daniil Orain's Moscow video](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)), you can see that cognitive tension played out in real time across several distinct layers.

---

### 1. Linguistic Insulation & Real-Time Filtering

When a microphone is pointed at someone in Moscow, the stakes of word choice are legally and physically real (laws against "discrediting the armed forces" or calling the conflict anything outside the official lexicon carry prison sentences).

You can observe respondents visibly wrestling with their phrasing:

* **Passive Constructions & Vague Subjects:** Instead of attributing actions to specific leaders, armies, or states, speakers default to phrases like *"всё это"* ("all this"), *"то, что происходит"* ("that which is happening" [[00:00:40](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]), or *"с любого военного мероприятия"* ("from any military event" [[00:03:43](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]).
* **Double-Hedging:** Notice how thoughts are introduced with multiple qualifiers before reaching an assertion: *"ну как сказать, ну не жуть, но..."* ("well, how to tell you, well, not horror, but..." [[00:03:05](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]) or *"я анализирую... ну как, я не могу сказать что я прямо анализирую..."* ("I analyze... well, I can't say I'm directly analyzing..." [[00:07:50](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]).
* **Explicit Boundaries:** Toward the end, one interviewee lays the tension bare: *"Разумеется, надеюсь, что это закончится быстро... более конкретно я свою позицию озвучивать не хочу"* ("Naturally, I hope this ends quickly... more specifically than that, I do not wish to voice my position" [[00:08:53](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]).

### 2. The Historical "Operational Heuristic" of Speech

This dynamic is an emergent survival mechanism honed over centuries of political repression:

* **Tsarist & Soviet Aesopian Language (*Эзопов язык*):** Originating under Tsarist censorship and perfected in Soviet intellectual life, public communication became an art of talking around the subject—using allegory, passive voice, abstract moralizing, and excessive preamble so that dissent was legible to peers but legally deniable to the state.
* **Doublethink as Everyday Grammar:** In public spaces, speech is treated not as a pure transmission of truth, but as a risk-management exercise. The cognitive load isn't just about *what* to say, but calculating *how an interrogator, employer, or passerby might interpret* every subordinate clause.
* **The "Neither/Nor" Neutralizer:** When forced to evaluate conflicting narratives, Russian speakers frequently adopt a performative agnosticism—e.g., *"the truth is somewhere in the middle, nobody is completely right"* [[00:07:56](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]. It fills the conversational void while signaling neutrality, fulfilling the social urge to reply without generating legal liability.

### 3. High Volume, Low Information Density

When someone wants to participate in a human interaction (answering an interviewer, talking to a neighbor) but cannot safely take a definitive stance, speech expands horizontally:

1. **Moral Platitudes:** *"War is bad, people dying is sad, everyone has families"* [[00:05:49](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]—universal, uncontroversial truths that fill dead air safely.
2. **Personal Coping Mechanisms:** Shifting the focus to psychology (*"I protect my nervous system,"* *"I just scroll past it"* [[00:02:16](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GcDi4wWmSk)]) rather than the structural reality causing the distress.
3. **Filler Particles:** Heavy use of *ну* (well), *как бы* (sort of / as it were), and *в общем* (generally) act as acoustic padding while the brain runs its internal censor.
